\textbf{Beat-level Alignment Score (BAS).}
Let $\mathcal{G} = \{k/4 : k \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}$ denote the
16th-note beat grid and $\varepsilon = 1/8$ beat ($\approx\!29\,\mathrm{ms}$
at 128\,BPM, half a 16th-note subdivision) the alignment tolerance.
For a generated sequence with $N$ note onsets $\{t_i\}$ (in beats):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bas}
  \mathrm{BAS} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \mathbf{1}\!\left[\min_{g\in\mathcal{G}} |t_i - g| < \varepsilon\right]
    \in [0,1].
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{BAS}=1$ indicates all notes are on-grid;
$\mathrm{BAS}=0.25$ corresponds to uniform random placement within a beat.
The metric captures \emph{perceptual rhythmic alignment}---
the degree to which the generated music ``locks to the grid'' as perceived
by the listener~\cite{toussaint2013geometry, mckay2006jsymbolic}---
complementing $\Delta_{\mathrm{sync}}$ (which measures continuous cross-track
timing error) with a musicologically-grounded binary grid-adherence measure.
